> These are **subjective estimates** made by skimming each problem statement (and, for Jane Street, the length/complexity of the published solution). They measure how hard the problem is to *solve from scratch*, not how long it is to read. Familiar “classics” are rated as if you have not seen them before.

## Scale

| Rating | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Trick question / riddle — answer in seconds once read carefully. |
| 2 | Very easy — one simple observation or short calculation. |
| 3 | Easy classic — standard interview warm-up; a few minutes. |
| 4 | Moderate — needs one clear idea (symmetry, linearity of expectation, pigeonhole, parity…). |
| 5 | Solid — typical quant-interview “hard” question; a non-obvious idea plus some care. |
| 6 | Hard — multi-step reasoning or a clever construction/invariant; good contest-level problem. |
| 7 | Very hard — olympiad-style insight, or a long logic/grid puzzle requiring sustained work. |
| 8 | Expert — deep insight or heavy computation; most strong solvers need hours (typical tough Jane Street month). |
| 9 | Elite — among the hardest here: few solvers crack it; very long, open-ended, or with obscure tricks. |
| 10 | Research-level — reserved; nothing here was rated 10. |
